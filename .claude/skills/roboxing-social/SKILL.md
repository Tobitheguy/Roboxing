# Roboxing social cards and brand

## What this skill is, and what it cannot do

This is a **specification**, not a renderer. It makes the judgment calls
consistent — layout, hierarchy, what a card is allowed to claim, how a caption
sounds. The images themselves are produced by **deterministic code**, not by a
model at post time.

That split is deliberate and non-negotiable:

- A card generated by a model at post time drifts. Two result cards a week
  apart come out with different margins, a different word for "decision", a
  different idea of where the logo goes. On a site whose entire proposition is
  that it is the reliable record, a feed that looks improvised undoes the thing
  the site is for.
- It also costs money per post and can fail at the moment it matters.

So: **templates live in `next/og` `ImageResponse` routes**, the same machinery
already rendering `src/app/opengraph-image.tsx`. This skill tells you how to
build and change them. When a card renders wrong, fix the template, not the
prompt.

## The brand, as it actually exists

Read the tokens from `src/app/globals.css` — never retype a hex value into a
template from memory. Current values, for orientation:

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-canvas` | `#f6f6f3` | page background (light) |
| `--color-ink` | `#14161a` | primary text, and the "volt" accent |
| `--color-ink-muted` | `#52565e` | secondary text |
| `--color-ink-dim` | `#6d717a` | tertiary, captions, credits |
| `--color-line` | `#e2e2dc` | hairlines |
| `--color-live` | `#e5231f` | **LIVE ONLY** |
| `--color-drift` | `#a35c00` | warning / behind the live edge |

Two rules that are easy to break and expensive to break:

1. **`--color-live` red means broadcasting. Nothing else.** Not "important",
   not "new", not a decorative accent on a result card. The moment red appears
   on a card about a finished fight, red stops meaning live.
2. **The accent is ink, not a colour.** This brand is monochrome on purpose.
   Emphasis comes from weight, size and the dark panel — not from adding a
   second hue. If a card needs to shout, invert it: ink background, canvas
   text. That is the "arena register" the home page spends its one dark panel
   on.

**Type:** `--font-display` is Oswald 700, condensed, uppercase for headings.
Body is the same family. Numbers use the `tabular` class so scores align.

**Logos:** `Logos/roboxing-r.svg` and `Logos/roboxing-r-black-square.svg`.
League marks are in `public/leagues/`. Note that the brand is **not settled** —
Tobias rejected an earlier name/logo/colour direction and is deciding himself.
Build templates so the mark and the palette come from one place each; a rebrand
must be a token change, not a rewrite of twelve templates.

## Fonts in an image route: bundle, never fetch

`src/app/opengraph-image.tsx` reads `src/app/_fonts/oswald-700.ttf` off disk
with `readFile`. Do the same in every new template.

A `fetch` to Google Fonts inside an image handler makes every render depend on
a third party's uptime, and the failure mode is a broken card on exactly the
post that travels. Satori cannot fall back gracefully — it renders the wrong
font or nothing.

## Formats

| Purpose | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Share / OG | 1200×630 | already built, three routes |
| Social square | 1080×1080 | X, Bluesky, Instagram single |
| Carousel / portrait | 1080×1350 | Instagram carousel, best feed real estate |
| Story / vertical | 1080×1920 | Stories, Shorts, TikTok covers |

Design the square first and let the portrait breathe — never the reverse. A
portrait design squashed into a square loses the bottom third, which is where
the source line goes.

## Card types, and what each must contain

Every card is generated from a **database row**, never from free text. If a
fact is not in the database, it does not go on a card.

**Result card** — robot A, robot B, method, round, the league mark, and the
confidence label. A result card without its confidence label is not shippable;
see the accuracy rules below.

**Fixture card** — event, league, date. If `date_tbd` is set, print
`date_label` and nothing else. Never render a placeholder instant as a date;
this is the same rule as `EventDate` on the site, and it exists because six
CyberHero circuit stops carry invented `starts_at` values so the calendar can
sort them.

**Open question card** — the question, one line of what is missing. These are
the strongest posts this account has, because nobody else tracks them: two
World Humanoid Robot Games with fighting as a scored event and no medallists
ever published; the most famous bout in the sport with no agreed winner.

**Machine card** — model, maker, the spec line, one photograph. Credit rules
below.

**Quote card** — a sentence from a named source, attributed on the card
itself. Never a paraphrase in quotation marks.

## Accuracy rules for anything that leaves the site

A post is harder to correct than a page. Treat a card as final.

- **The confidence label travels with the fact.** `confirmed` needs no badge;
  `reported` and `unconfirmed` must be labelled on the card, not only in the
  caption. A screenshot of a card outlives its caption.
- **Never state a winner the record marks unconfirmed.** The URKL opener is the
  live example — Wikipedia says Matador, Newsweek reads as White Eagle, the
  promoter published nothing. A card claiming either is a card we have to
  delete.
- **No invented numbers.** No attendance, no viewership, no "biggest ever", no
  rankings the database does not compute.
- **Link out in the caption**, always, to the page the card was generated from.
  The card is the claim; the page is the evidence.

## Photography and credit

`public/machines/` and `public/leagues/` hold manufacturer and league
photography, every file credited in `src/lib/machine-media.ts`.

Using those editorially on an article page is one thing. Putting the same
photograph on a branded social card, where the credit line is small or absent
and the framing implies it is ours, is a different act.

Therefore:

- A photo on a card **must** carry its credit, legibly, on the card itself —
  bottom edge, `--color-ink-dim`, small but not hidden.
- Prefer **type-led cards** with no photography. They are more distinctive in a
  feed than a manufacturer's press shot that four other accounts posted today,
  and they carry no rights question at all.
- **Never** put a league's own video frame or broadcast still on a card.

## Caption voice

Same voice as the site: plain, past tense, specific, no hype.

- State the fact first. The hook is that the fact is surprising, not that the
  sentence is exciting.
- No exclamation marks, no "BREAKING", no emoji strings, no "you won't
  believe".
- Name the source when the fact is single-sourced: "reported by Xinhua's
  English service".
- Say what is NOT known when that is the story. "Nobody has published the
  medallists" is a stronger post than a medal table would be.
- End with the link.

Good: *"Team FBA beat Team Al Majd 4–3 over seven rounds to win the first
CyberHero event. The decider was a knockdown from a kick. The team names rest
on Xinhua alone — no other outlet has published them."*

Bad: *"🔥 INSANE ROBOT KO! You HAVE to see this 🤖👊"*

## Where the code goes

- Existing share cards: `src/app/**/opengraph-image.tsx`
- Social card templates: put them under `src/app/api/social/` as routes that
  take an entity slug and a format, so a card can be regenerated from a URL and
  reviewed before it is posted.
- Never inline copy into a template. Every word comes from the row being
  rendered, for the same reason the entry-route tiles carry no prose: text
  written into a component cannot be corrected when the fact changes.
